Ledger, calmer

Round four held up against long names, but every row said everything at once. This round applies progressive disclosure: each level answers one question and leaves detail to the next tap. Data and stress cases are the same as before: 14 people, long names, two members called Ana, two payers, an uneven split.

One question per levelGroup: “where do I stand?” Expense list: “what happened?” Expense: “how was it split?” Each level shows only its own answer, and the rest is one tap deeper.
You first, everyone else afterYour balance and your next step lead the group. The full plan to settle everyone, and who else is settled, fold away until asked for.
Recognition, not readingPayers become a coloured initial instead of a name, and a green dot shows who is online. Expenses that leave you out are dimmed rather than labelled “not in it”.
Defaults over choicesA new expense opens with the common case: paid by you, split equally between everyone. Shares, amounts and the per-person list only appear once you change the split.
One figure per rowRows carry a name and one amount. Your share sits under an expense’s amount only when it is not zero. Bars, split types and counts move to the levels where they help.
Fewer words in the chromeInvite, add a person and Activity become header icons. Search hides behind an icon until you need it. There are two tabs, not three, and Settle up becomes an action beside your balance.
