Ledger, with realistic content

Round three’s F, redrawn with no convenient data. Names run to three words or use hyphens and diacritics, two members are both called Ana, groups and expenses have long names, amounts reach five figures, and the group still has 14 people and 126 expenses. The last screen is a 320px phone. These rules decide where every long thing goes:

Amounts never shrink or truncateFigures sit in a column sized to their content, and text gives way to them. A big amount in the editor gets its own line, never sharing one with a description.
Names wrap to two lines, then end in “…”This applies to members, groups, expenses and the screen title. A long name makes its row taller, while its bar and amount stay aligned.
Only secondary text is cut to one lineWho paid and the split each keep a fixed slot: the payer’s name gets cut first, so “· 13 of 14” always stays visible.
Several names are summed up, not listed“Benedikt S.-H. and Christopher-Alexander” becomes “Benedikt Schwarzenberger-H… +1”. The full list is in the expense itself.
Two names in one row get two linesIn Settle up, the payer goes on the first line and “→ recipient” on the second, so neither name has to shrink to fit beside the other.
Duplicate names get a muted ordinalThe second Ana shows as “Ana (2)”, as CONTEXT.md describes. You always show as “You”, so your own name never takes a row’s width.
Back links shrink and actions stay put“‹ Interrail summer 2026 with…” ends in “…” before any header icon moves. Tabs never wrap; their counts are small figures.
Bars sit under the text, never behind itEach balance bar is a 3px strip along the foot of its row, growing from a centre tick. A name of any length can cross the middle without a line running through it.
Pinned parts stay one lineThe Add expense button, the assigned-total footer and the tabs keep a fixed height, so the space for the list is predictable.
